How Long Is Now?

An interactive experiment in clock time, lived time, and remembered time.

Updated 2026-09-08 Phase 02 · all ten scenes run Home github.com/adamdhickey-collab/how-long-is-now

The next step

Watch it, end to end. As of 2026-09-08 all ten scenes have content and the piece runs continuously from the first frame to the last: the held second, ten minutes, a day, the year, the lifetime, the turn and its fall, the descent into one second, the two clocks, the memory corridor, and the return, which ends by taking the interface away. Nothing between here and a finished piece is a scene that does not exist.

What that means is the gate’s question is finally askable, and it is not a question code can answer: sit down and scroll the whole thing at full size. The two places to watch are the turn — whether the black is long enough and the fall lands — and the two clocks, where the argument either arrives without a word or does not. Everything after depends on what that look says.

Since then the park itself has changed direction (2026-09-08, ledger session 10): the reference is a pointillist Lake Harriet alive with people, and every scene that holds the park now sits on its lawn. The year’s autumn, winter and spring followed the same day as edits of the late-summer layers (ledger 10b). The procedural part followed on the same day: the season keyframes’ sky, water, haze and cloud colours regraded to the reference — a bright pale-horizoned blue, cobalt water, a cool pale haze, ice in winter — and the sky and the water laid down in a screen-space stipple the size of the plates’ dots, so they sit with the painted layers instead of beside them.

A first pass of that look was made by the agent on 2026-09-08, at full size and at a phone’s width, every scene at several points: it found and fixed a caption that a fast scroll through its window could leave stuck up, the elm’s rings outliving the lifetime, and the opening frame waiting on all 7.5 MB of imagery before it showed. What it could not judge is the thing the gate asks. At a phone’s width the year’s survey is legible but crowded — the specimen block sits over the bench and the callouts at the analemma collide — which is a layout decision for the figure, not a bug.

Known unmade, in the order the piece would miss them: the rest of the small life of the opening second — a leaf that moves, a bicycle that passes — the bench and the blink being in as of 2026-09-08; the spectrogram and the hand, which are scene 07’s and wait partly on sound; sound itself, which CONCEPT.md says matters almost as much as the visuals; and the performance budget, which was first measured on 2026-09-08 (phase 05) and holds on the machine it was built on, with the phone still unmeasured on a phone.

01

The concept film

Park decided · film deferred

A 45–60 second film of the whole arc: park → expanding time → lifetime → crash back → inward time → memory corridor → park. Crude is fine — stills, Fable motion studies, generated video, rough type. This is the visual screenplay; every scene in the manifest gets its look decided here, cheaply, before any of it is engineered.

  • Decide the park — Lake Harriet, Minneapolis. Its four seasons are declared in the manifest as colour, light height, leaf density and what falls through the air.
  • Fable: type studies for the HUD, the scale labels, the two closing lines.
  • Generated plates: the park across seasons — pipeline proven in ASSETS.md; scene 04’s canopy exists in all four seasons.
  • Scene 01, the park at one second — opened 2026-09-08: the year’s world held at its opening frame through the manifest’s new hold, seen from the bench with a still camera, the thermal reading arriving on its own time as the first thing noticed. As of 2026-09-08 the bench is in and so is the blink: the viewer’s own eye, once, five and a half seconds into the second, the lids closing over everything the eye sees, the interface included, and opening again — declared in the manifest, skipped under reduced motion. On 2026-09-08 the seat moved from the bench to a blanket on the lawn among people, in the reference’s pointillist park: the elms overhead, the couple and the reader and the man with his dog just ahead, sitters across the lawn, sails on the lake, and one jogger passing on the path in real time — the small life of the second. The other walkers wait for scene 02. A leaf that moves is still to make.
  • Scene 02, the ten minutes — opened 2026-09-08: the same hold with a span of the world’s time, so scroll runs ten minutes of the park’s own motion — clouds, air, water, the sun’s shadows — under a still camera, while the world starts to be read: the wind on the water, then the air through the radar. People crossing the frame are still to make.
  • Scene 03, the day — opened 2026-09-08: the hold runs a full day of the world’s clock through a lens of its own so the sun can set; the year’s world gained a night from the real sun’s altitude, a dusk that warms the horizon, and a thermal that swings with the light; the day’s arc is drawn as it happens.
  • Scene 05, the lifetime — opened 2026-09-08: the year’s world held at the August it closed on, the camera rising and drawing back as eighty years pass; the elms grow, the sun’s record stacks a figure a year into a band, and an elm counts the years as rings; the world fades for the turn. As of 2026-09-08 the far shore is cut open too: the stand’s roots drawn as a network under it, one hairline a root, each growing from its start to its tip with the years and the trees joining underground late, as a stand’s do. People as appearances and the changing shore are still to make.
  • Scene 06, the turn — opened 2026-09-08: everything stops. Black, then the line, then the fall — the park comes back through a windowed camera move that gathers speed and lands gently at the bench, the world arriving out of the dark halfway down.
  • Scene 10, the return — opened 2026-09-08: exactly the same lake from exactly the same seat at exactly the same second, the camera still, because nothing about the afternoon has changed and only what is noticed has. Every instrument faintly on at once, then gone one by one — the ring of seasons first, the wind on the water last — and the same eye blinking in the same second, on the same count as at the opening (2026-09-08); then the line, and then the interface itself: the label, the hint and the clock fade and do not come back, and the piece ends with nothing between the viewer and the park.
  • Scene 07, attention — opened 2026-09-08: the fall’s landing, held. The clock does not move and neither does the world; what changes is how finely the second is divided — the label descends from one second to a hundredth without leaving the scene — and how much of what was always there is being read, the instruments arriving one after another and none of them leaving, so it ends with every layer on at once. As of 2026-09-08 the finest division has its figure: one elm leaf read as the network it is, its venation drawn vein by vein and lit node by node into a scope at the frame’s foot as the label descends to a hundredth of a second, still being drawn when the corridor takes the frame. The spectrogram and the thermal of one hand are still to make; the first waits on sound.

Taken out of order on purpose: scene 04 was built first so the film has a working world to shoot against, and so the pipeline’s cost was known before a screenplay committed to it.

02

Three finished moments

Moment 1 under way

Built one at a time, each to done — beautiful, responsive, legible with almost no copy:

  • The park → one year. The pipeline proof: plates + parallax + scroll choreography + the fixed clock. Choreography built; every plate drawn in the decided style across four seasons; the instruments are what remain.
  • Ten identical minutes. The two corridors, lived vs. remembered — the centerpiece interaction. Opened 2026-09-06: the manifest declares the corridor, the two clocks’ travel, the turn and the fragments; the scene renders one procedural corridor twice, side by side, and reverses at the turn. The corridor is drawn: one bay each of wall, ceiling and floor, tiled along it, and eighteen fragment cutouts on the panes (ledger session 7); on 2026-09-08 all of it was drawn again in the park’s pointillist style (session 11). The waiting clock has its one bare instrument, a seconds dial at the end of its corridor that jumps a second at a time as you scroll. The absorbed clock is read through its layers while it happens, a thermal reading with isotherms, motes in the lamplight, and a survey of measurements that thickens as the minutes pass, and looking back the densities swap. What remains for moment 2 is the look at the whole, end to end.
  • The memory corridor. Thirty days as panes of glass; alignment vs. scatter. Opened 2026-09-08: the manifest declares the days, the eye and its long lens, and two months with their windows, scatter and readings; the scene stacks a month of the same day into one sheet and scatters a month of different days across the frame, a dimension counting the days under each. The panes carry the sixty squares (ledger session 8): nine desks the same month reuses, thirty different days for the other; on 2026-09-08 all five sheets were drawn again in the park’s pointillist style (session 12), and with that every plate in the piece is in one style. What remains for moment 3 is the look at the whole, end to end.

The gate: if these three moments work, the project exists. If they don’t, stop — nothing else gets built on top of them.

Connective tissue

2–4 weeks

The remaining scenes in the manifest — 10 seconds, the day, the lifetime, the turn, the inward zoom, the return — plus the transitions between all ten. By now the pipeline is proven and each scene is bounded work an agent can be pointed at.

04

Sound

1–2 weeks

One environmental soundscape, transformed as time changes: birds become textures, footsteps become pulses, years collapse into noise, the inward zoom isolates single sounds. Web Audio, driven from the same manifest. A visible on/off control from day one.

05

Polish and launch

1–2 weeks
  • Performance to budget: 60fps mid-range laptop, 30fps floor on phone, first frame under 3s. First measured 2026-09-08 on the machine the piece is built on (M5 Pro, 1728×907 at 2×): every scene holds the display’s 120 Hz with no frame over 10 ms, the loop’s own work is under 0.3 ms a frame in every scene, and the page first paints at about 60 ms. The opening frame’s imagery — the four late-summer plates, 1.7 MB — is now fetched alone ahead of the other 5.8 MB, so on a slow connection the park shows about four times sooner than it did. At a phone’s resolution (375×812 at 2×, emulated on the same machine) a whole frame, GPU included, costs under 0.5 ms in every scene, which leaves a phone’s slower GPU an order of magnitude of room under the 30 fps floor. A mid-range laptop and a phone have still not been measured on themselves.
  • Reduced-motion: degrades to a legible still essay, never breaks.
  • Playwright screenshot states across breakpoints; mobile scroll feel.
  • Then: Awwwards / FWA submission, and the piece linked from adamhickey.com.

The rules that keep it buildable

Cheat like crazy. Choreography over polygon count. Video, plates, and depth maps wherever live 3-D isn’t earning its cost. The viewer must not be able to tell where video stops and 3-D begins.
The manifest owns time. All choreography flows from src/scenes/manifest.ts. Nothing outside it invents timing — which is also what lets an AI agent work one bounded scene without rebuilding the project.
Three moments before the universe. No connective tissue until the three hardest moments are finished and beautiful. The gate exists so three weeks aren’t spent building a universe around an idea that doesn’t land.
Tools stay in their lane. Three.js/GSAP own the experience. Fable is the motion sketchbook. Generative models manufacture ingredients. Nothing generative owns the website.
